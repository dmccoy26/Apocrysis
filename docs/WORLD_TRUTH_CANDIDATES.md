# World truth — candidates

Written 2026-08-29 for the `APOCRYSIS_ROADMAP.md` §10 decision: the
campaign's authored truth must be ratified before the `WorldFact` DAG
can be written. Three distinct candidates below. Pick one, or name the
elements to combine.

> **FULL SPOILERS.** This is the designer's bible. Do not read if you
> intend to play the campaign blind.

Scope for all three: **~25 expeditions, 5 chapters + a finale**
(roadmap §4/§9). Each is structured so the player's early conclusions
are *earned and wrong*, disproved in four stages, and each resolves
three threads (the Disappearance / the Dead / the Response) into one
operation seen from three angles.

---

## Candidate A — "The Cordon"

**Logline:** the evacuation was real. So was the decision to seal the
region on a schedule and leave everyone still inside.

### What happened
A contained neurological pathogen — tied to a regional research or
agricultural containment station — escapes during a transfer. It
spreads faster than any model. A regional emergency command activates
a staged evacuation ("Protocol Seven"): consolidation points, a few
signed corridors, convoys out. Then, on a timetable fixed from the
start, the cordon seals — corridors close, comms are cut — while tens
of thousands are still inside, told help is coming. Regional command
goes dark. The wider world is intact; it contained the outbreak by
sealing the region off and letting it go quiet.

### The apocalypse-proper
Not the infection — **the response**. The plan worked exactly as
designed. The horror is bureaucratic: a system that sacrificed a
population to protect itself, on paper, with signatures.

### The Dead
The region's own people — those who didn't make the corridors, plus
the original contained cases. Early infected were lucid and declining
slowly; late infected are what the player fights. The difference is
**disease stage**, not kind.

### The wrong-assumptions ladder
1. *Everyone was killed by the infected.* → organised-evacuation
   evidence disproves it.
2. *Everyone evacuated.* → the corridors closed with people inside.
3. *The military got them out.* → partly; the same body sealed the
   cordon.
4. *The evacuation was a rescue.* → the seal was scheduled from the
   start. Rescue and abandonment were one operation.

### Hidden timeline
`-40` contained incident · `-14` first hospital cases · `-10`
neurological reports · `-7` services overwhelmed · `-5` Protocol Seven
· `-3` corridors open · `-2` comms cut (deliberate) · `-1` corridors
close, still-full · `0` command dark, the Silence · `+1` survivor
wakes.

### Chapters
1. THE SILENCE — why is everyone gone?
2. THE INFECTED — where did it start? why do early/late differ?
3. THE EVACUATION — where did the corridors lead?
4. THE RESPONSE — who ordered the seal, and when was it decided?
5. THE LAST SIGNAL — is anyone still alive?
FIN. THE TRUTH — the regional command centre.

### Endgame
The command centre holds the seal order and the signature. The last
transmission points to a consolidation point that **held** — a walled
settlement of people who were told to keep walking and instead stayed
and defended. The final act: reach the first living humans. The
choice: the cordon still has ears — broadcast the truth outward, or
protect the settlement's silence.

### Register / theme
Institutional betrayal. Grief and anger. "It worked as designed."

### DAG notes
~28 `WorldFact`s, threads evenly split. Leans hard on **corroborative**
mysteries (two records that together prove the seal was scheduled) and
**informational** (transmission provenance) — good fit for the
knowledge model's under-used `Deduction`. Milestones: M1 organised, M2
regional-only, M3 comms cut on purpose, M5 corridors closed on
schedule, M6 same command both orders, M8 still transmitting, M9
living people.

### Risks / engine demands
Needs the corroboration gate (roadmap §3.2) landed early — the truth
doesn't work without "two independent records agree." Endgame settlement
wants at least one NPC-adjacent beat (can be a static group + dialogue-
free scene; doesn't need the full living-world architecture).

## Candidate B — "The Quiet"

**Logline:** nobody was evacuated. Over two weeks, everyone walked
north on their own — calmly, deliberately — toward something. You're
the one who didn't go.

### What happened
Not a pathogen first. A phenomenon — call it the drift: a pull, north,
toward a single site (a research installation, or something older
under it). It begins as restlessness, then people start leaving.
Not fleeing — walking, unhurried, in the same direction, and not
coming back. Authorities try to stop it: quarantine, road blockades
north, detainment. They fail. "Phase Two" was the order to **stop
trying**. The infection is the terminal state of *resisting* the drift
too long, or of being the last one left when everyone who could follow
it already has.

### The apocalypse-proper
A mass migration with an unknown attractor. Every checkpoint,
evacuation notice, and military log the player finds is a **failed
attempt to hold people back**. The player is the anomaly — the one the
pull didn't take.

### The Dead
People who didn't go: who stayed for someone, were restrained, or were
too far gone to walk. Early infected still **face north**. Late
infected have forgotten why.

### The wrong-assumptions ladder
1. *Everyone was killed or evacuated.* → no bodies, no convoys; the
   roads north are worn smooth by foot traffic *leaving*.
2. *They fled something.* → they weren't running. The blockades face
   the wrong way — built to stop people going *in* that direction.
3. *The military moved them north.* → the military tried to **stop**
   them and lost.
4. *Something drove them out.* → something **called** them, and it's
   still calling. You just can't hear it.

### Hidden timeline
`-16` first "restlessness" clinic notes · `-12` people begin leaving,
singly · `-9` northbound road traffic spikes, all outbound · `-6`
quarantine declared · `-4` northern blockades built · `-3` blockades
overrun from the *south* side · `-2` "Phase Two" — cease interdiction ·
`-1` the last organised bodies (police, hospital staff) go · `0` the
Silence · `+1` survivor wakes.

### Chapters
1. THE SILENCE — where is everyone?
2. THE PULL — which way did they go, and why that way?
3. THE RESISTANCE — the failed blockades; who fought it and lost?
4. THE SITE — what is north?
5. THE LAST ONES — the people who stayed; what happened to them?
FIN. THE TRUTH — the attractor.

### Endgame
The last transmission is from the site itself — a looped fragment, or
a voice that knows you're coming. The player reaches it and finds
**the people**. All of them. Gathered, alive or something adjacent,
around whatever called them. The choice: understand it and walk in, or
turn around as the last person in the world who chose not to.

### Register / theme
Folk-horror. Eerie, quiet, awed. Loneliness rather than betrayal:
you missed a decision the whole species made.

### DAG notes
~26 `WorldFact`s. Leans on **directional** and **sequential**
mysteries (follow the drift, reconstruct the route north) and
**informational** late (what the site is). Milestones: M1 they walked,
M2 all one direction, M3 blockades faced inward, M4 the pull predates
the infection, M6 interdiction was abandoned, M8 the site is
transmitting, M9 they're all still there.

### Risks / engine demands
The "everyone walked the same way" reveal wants **map-v2's topology
awareness** to land sooner — the roads-worn-smooth, blockades-face-the-
wrong-way evidence reads much better on an authored valley than a
random grid. The ending is a single designed location and a mood
piece; low mechanical demand but high writing bar. Least "gamey" of
the three — closest to a walking-sim finale.

## Candidate C — "The Handoff"

**Logline:** the evacuation succeeded. Almost everyone got out. Then
the region was handed over — and it's still in use.

### What happened
A fast, ugly, but **survivable** outbreak — contained within a month,
far fewer dead than feared. The region was genuinely, successfully
evacuated. Then it wasn't abandoned: it was **repurposed**. The
agency that ran the evacuation kept the region as an asset — the
infected population maintained and studied, the fences kept up, zones
culled, observation posts manned. Someone has been coming and going
the entire time. The player is a variable in a long-running study,
and has been let live this long **on purpose**.

### The apocalypse-proper
Not the outbreak, and not an abandonment — an **ongoing operation**.
The world moved on and turned the player's home into a facility. The
infected are inventory. The player is data.

### The Dead
The outbreak's victims, now a **managed population**. Sites near active
operations show deliberate feeding, containment, culling. Infected near
those sites are calmer — **habituated**.

### The wrong-assumptions ladder
1. *I'm the last person here.* → resupplied caches, fresh fence
   repairs, recent tire tracks.
2. *Scavengers come here.* → the caches are catalogued; the culls are
   scheduled; this is organised.
3. *They come for salvage.* → they come **for the infected** —
   observation logs, sample manifests, a cull rota.
4. *They come for the infected.* → they come **for me**. Camera posts.
   A file with your movements in it. You've been observed since Day 1.

### Hidden timeline
`-21` first cases · `-14` outbreak peak · `-9` evacuation begins
(orderly) · `-2` region cleared, ~96% out · `0` cordon established,
the Silence · `+1` survivor wakes · **`+3` first cache resupply** ·
**`+11` first cull** · **`+26` first observation-post rotation** — the
operation's clock runs *forward* from Day 0, and the player's own
expeditions are on it.

### Chapters
1. THE SILENCE — where did everyone go?
2. THE INFECTED — what were they, and what are they now?
3. THE EVACUATION — it worked. so what's the region *for*?
4. THE ACTIVITY — who's been here since, and why?
5. THE WATCHERS — how long have they been watching *me*?
FIN. THE TRUTH — an active operational channel; a staffed post.

### Endgame
The last transmission is a **live** operational channel — not a loop.
The command post is staffed *now*. The player has been an observed
variable (in immunity? in survival behaviour? in the infected? in the
player specifically?). The choice: accept extraction and become a data
point, expose the operation to whatever's outside the cordon, or
vanish back into the region and stay off the ledger.

### Register / theme
Paranoid thriller. Dread and violation. The horror is being *seen* —
and being small enough to write off and study.

### DAG notes
~27 `WorldFact`s. Leans on **experimental** (which cull valve / which
containment protocol) and **time-pressure** (the operation's forward
clock — reach a site before the next rotation clears it). Milestones:
M1 people come here, M2 it's organised, M3 they come for the infected,
M4 the operation post-dates Day 0, M6 there's a file on you, M8 the
channel is live, M9 the post is staffed right now.

### Risks / engine demands
The forward-running operation clock wants **time-pressure** mechanics
mature and a **world-conditions / scheduled-event** system (roadmap
Phase D) sooner than the other two. Highest mechanical demand. Also the
most tonally different from what's built — the current prose is
elegiac; this wants tension. Biggest rewrite of voice.

## CHOSEN — A "The Cordon" (2026-08-29)

World truth **A** locked in `PHASE_A_DECISIONS.md` (CH1+CH2 `WorldFact`s
already authored from it). **Ending: authored-canonical + one final
binary choice** — the command centre holds the seal order; the last
transmission leads to a consolidation point that *held*; the final act
is reaching the first living humans; the single choice is **broadcast
the truth outward past the cordon vs protect the settlement's silence**.
Both branches authored; no investigation-gated multi-ending.

## Open sub-decisions (A only; resolve during CH3–FIN authoring)

- **The cause specifics.** Research vs agricultural containment station.
- **How much the wider world matters.** The cordon "still has ears" —
  the broadcast branch needs the wider world to be reachable/real
  enough for that choice to land. Decide the minimum: is it a genuine
  transmission that gets out, or an act of witness that may not?
- ~~The ending: fixed or player choice?~~ **DECIDED — fixed narrative,
  one final binary choice.**
- **Tone.** A stays in the current elegiac register — no voice rewrite.

## Recommendation

**A — The Cordon**, unless you specifically want to move the game's
tone. It fits the existing voice, its "two records that agree" spine
forces the `Deduction` half of the knowledge model to finally do
work (which the roadmap wants anyway), and its engine demand
(corroboration gate) is already the top-ranked Phase-5 item in
`SCENARIO_EXPANSION.md`. B is the most artistically distinctive and
the lowest mechanical risk but the highest writing bar and the least
game-like finale. C is the most exciting premise and the biggest
build — it needs Phase D pulled forward and a voice rewrite.
